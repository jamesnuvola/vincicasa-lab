# Q141-A8 — power and endpoint audit (2026-11-13 06:43 Europe/Rome)

**Status:** completed design simulation; not a predictive test.  
**B11 status:** one forecast frozen for contest 317; zero official outcomes reconciled.  
**No B11 outcome, historical target outcome, or future result was read or used.**

## 1. Why this audit was necessary

The A8 draft proposes 200 future targets, at least 50 divergence-active targets, a conditional paired Top-5 hit-share improvement of +0.020, and an unconditional improvement of +0.005. It also proposes ten non-overlapping blocks of 20 and an exact two-sided sign-flip test over all 1,024 block-sign configurations, with Holm correction over two endpoints.

Before activating this draft, I checked whether that design can detect its own minimum effect.

## 2. Exact null variance

Let `m` be the number of replacements between the two Top-5 sets (`m=1` means 4/5 overlap; `m=5` means disjoint sets). For a uniform 5-of-40 draw, define

`D = (hits in M1-only numbers - hits in M0-only numbers) / 5`.

The exact hypergeometric variance is

`Var(D | m) = [2 * 5 * m / 40 * (40 - 5) / 39] / 25`.

This variance rises with `m`: more membership divergence also creates a noisier paired hit-share difference.

## 3. Exact block-sign-flip sensitivity simulation

For each `m=1..5`, I enumerated the exact 5-of-40 distribution of the hit-count difference, then created a sensitivity alternative by exponentially tilting that distribution so that the active-target mean paired Top-5 hit-share difference is exactly +0.020. The tilt is a mathematical power scenario, not a claim about the lottery.

For each scenario:
- 20,000 Monte Carlo datasets;
- 200 total targets arranged in ten blocks of 20;
- either 50 active targets (five per block) or 200 active targets (twenty per block);
- all 1,024 block-sign configurations enumerated per dataset;
- raw alpha 0.025, the conservative Holm worst-case threshold for two registered endpoints;
- separate null simulations to check rejection-rate calibration.

| Replacements m | Top-5 overlap | Power, 50 active | Null rejection, 50 | Power, 200 active | Null rejection, 200 | IID normal n80 active |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4/5 | 6.29% | 0.37% | 51.27% | 1.18% | 213 |
| 2 | 3/5 | 4.79% | 0.95% | 26.97% | 1.57% | 427 |
| 3 | 2/5 | 3.74% | 0.97% | 17.91% | 1.67% | 640 |
| 4 | 1/5 | 3.48% | 1.23% | 13.99% | 1.68% | 853 |
| 5 | 0/5 | 3.02% | 1.36% | 11.07% | 1.77% | 1,066 |

Monte Carlo standard errors for power estimates range from approximately 0.12 to 0.35 percentage points. Under the null, simulated rejection rates were 0.37%–1.78%, below the nominal 2.5% threshold because the ten-block exact test is discrete and conservative.

**Main result:** under the stated +0.020 alternative, the registered ten-block test has estimated power of only 3.0%–6.3% when there are 50 active targets. Even if all 200 targets are active, estimated power ranges from 11.1% to 51.3%. The most favorable case (`m=1`, all 200 active) is still below 80%.

The IID normal approximation, which ignores the ten-block discreteness and is therefore optimistic, suggests 213–1,066 active targets for 80% power across `m=1..5`. At a 25% divergence prevalence this corresponds to roughly 852–4,265 total targets, before any allowance for serial dependence.

## 4. Endpoint and protocol ambiguity

Because the paired Top-5 difference is exactly zero whenever the two Top-5 sets coincide, the unconditional mean is algebraically linked to the conditional mean:

`mean_all = (number_active / 200) * mean_active`.

The two effect-size thresholds are therefore not independent pieces of evidence. Under a block-sum sign-flip implementation, both endpoints also produce the same sign-flip ordering and hence the same raw p-value (only the scale differs). The current A8 draft does not explicitly state whether each block contributes a sum or a mean. That must be specified before activation.

This audit does **not** alter the draft's thresholds or B11. It records a design limitation so that A8 is not mistakenly treated as adequately powered.

## 5. Decision

- Keep A8 **DRAFT ONLY**; do not activate the current 200-target design as a confirmatory test.
- Do not change B11, its frozen prediction, or its 200-target protocol.
- Before any future A8 activation, choose one confirmatory endpoint, define the block statistic explicitly, and either increase the prospective sample substantially or use a more information-efficient pre-calibrated proper scoring rule with a separate preregistration.
- Do not reuse B11 outcomes to tune or validate A8.

## 6. Reproducibility and limits

- Six unit tests passed.
- The full 10-scenario audit was run twice with identical JSON output.
- All ten null-calibration rates were at or below 2.5%.
- The simulation uses only combinatorial counts and synthetic draws; no historical or prospective VinciCasa outcomes.
- This is a design/power audit, not Discovery/Validation/Blind OOS evidence and not evidence that either model predicts lottery numbers.
